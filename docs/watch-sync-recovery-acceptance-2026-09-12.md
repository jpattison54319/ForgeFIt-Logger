# Watch recovery — build 99

The later queue-replay and HR-delay report required additional changes. See the [delivery hardening report](watch-delivery-hardening-2026-09-12.md) for the current implementation and validation; the evidence below describes the first recovery pass.

Build 99 implements the recovery changes identified in the [source audit](watch-sync-recovery-audit-2026-09-12.md). The original physical workout's initiating failure is still unconfirmed. Equal version/build labels do not establish identical installed source or installation channel.

## Behavior

- The phone activates WatchConnectivity during app initialization, including a blocked persistence launch. A correlated handshake reads committed workout data through a fresh ModelContext and returns an explicit storage or protocol error when it cannot provide a graph.
- Snapshots carry a phone process epoch, increasing revision, workout identity, protocol version, and peer build information. Cold recovery, a new phone epoch, and a new HealthKit handoff require a reply to the current request. Stale revisions and obsolete replies cannot replace current state.
- An active-only liveness probe requests verified state after 30 seconds without an accepted snapshot. It is canceled on Stop and idle. This covers silently lost terminal/context updates without waiting for a reachability edge.
- Recovery retries use delays of 3, 6, 12, 20, then 30 seconds. Idle recovery stops after six attempts; foreground/reachability changes and the visible Retry control can restart it. An active unresolved workout continues at the capped interval.
- The Watch exposes Retry Sync and a confirmed Stop on Watch during interrupted recording. Stop cancels outstanding recovery, discards only the local recorder, and preserves the phone workout. Resume on Watch is explicit. A bounded local suppression ledger survives relaunch and prevents a late message from restarting that workout. Idle connection errors do not expose Stop.
- Finish and discard messages are scoped to a workout in both directions. Bounded terminal identities prevent a late active snapshot from resurrecting an already ended workout. A durable local reset outbox survives inactive transport and is flushed after activation. Account reset invalidates its prior phone epoch; a delayed notice cannot clear a newly verified epoch. A reset from an unknown epoch requests verification rather than guessing which workout to erase.
- HealthKit recovery retries after temporary bootstrap errors. Recorder restart attempts retain their per-workout budget across ordinary snapshots, with backoff and explicit recovery for authorization, another recorder, or an exhausted budget. Same-workout automatic retries retain accumulated zone time. Teardown has a five-second escape if collection completion never returns; late callbacks are guarded by session object and lifecycle generation.
- Unknown optional presentation enums no longer prevent workout graph decoding. Workout identity and structural data remain strict. A bounded device-local diagnostic history stores operational event names, error domain/code, and peer version information; it contains no workout payloads or health measurements, is not exported in backup preferences, and is explicitly cleared by account reset.

Watch Finish remains visibly in progress until HealthKit reports its save result; repeated Finish/Discard/Retry cannot launch competing operations. A delayed save does not prematurely show a completion summary or guess that a phone fallback is safe. An incoming idle or next-workout snapshot cannot cancel or replace that in-flight recorder; completion reconciles the most recent phone state. Metrics from a different workout cannot claim HealthKit save ownership. A successful nil workout result (possible while locked) is treated as success, per [Apple's finishWorkout contract](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/healthkit/hkworkoutbuilder/finishworkout(completion:)).

Covers delayed finish/discard A after B, late recovery after Stop, relaunch suppression, terminal resurrection, provisional handoff binding, three failed recoveries followed by recovery, invalid payloads, idle Stop refusal, canceled replies, delayed reset notices, a liveness probe after a silently missed phone finish, and delayed HealthKit completion without premature UI teardown. - Phone app: 24 tests across handshake, terminal identity, structured set sync, and custom increment preferences passed (`/tmp/forgefit-watch99-phone-final.xcresult`). The handshake test verifies an isolated committed finish is observed through a fresh context. ## Review and limits

Tree-lint findings were reviewed: page/list container labels and partially offscreen scroll rows are informational; custom recovery controls have visible labels and 55.5-point button heights. Native watchOS confirmation exposes its text as a 120×24 AX button inside a 211×53 action cell; the native action is hittable and was activated successfully. The system Close button is 37×37. No custom smaller touch target was introduced.

The independent acceptance reviewer is `gpt-5.6-luna`, verified in the bundled model catalog. Initial review findings about reset clearing, idle Stop, canceled retries, teardown timeout, blocked-storage activation, and metrics error diagnostics were addressed and covered by focused tests where platform injection permits it. The final review signed off after the Finish ownership race was corrected and both context transitions passed; no further actionable findings remained in the requested source scope.

The deterministic UI fixture does not start HealthKit or establish physical WatchConnectivity delivery. Simulator success does not prove wrist-down live HR, physical sensor ownership, a missing system completion callback, or paired end-to-end finish propagation. Phone and Watch installation and launch are separate checks from these tests. A process death during an unresolved HealthKit save can still leave the phone workout active. A fully durable cross-device pending-save protocol is a separate follow-up; guessing failure at a timeout could duplicate a save that actually succeeded. No Cloud/TestFlight upload or release publication is part of this validation.

Disk recovery also removed completed simulator test object/binary products, regenerable compiler caches, and 6,313 individually audited HTTP cache / downloaded Homebrew bottle files older than one day (919,605,120 logical bytes). Chrome profile storage, active updater staging, simulator data, source files, and validation artifacts were preserved. Free space reached 2.1 GiB before the final builds and measured 1.2 GiB after the installable build.
